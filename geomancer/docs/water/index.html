Geomancer: Water

Geomancer: Water analyses your landscape's elevation and places ocean, lakes and rivers where the terrain says they belong — as native Unreal WaterBody actors you can edit like any others.

Geomancer: Water 1.0 · Verified 8 August 2026

It is editor-only. Nothing compiles into a packaged build.

You need: Unreal Engine 5.8, a landscape with collision in your level, and the Water and Landscape Patch plugins — both of which enable automatically when you install Geomancer.

Your first five minutes

1. Open the panel. Tools → Geomancer → Geomancer: Water. It is not under the Window menu — Tools is the only route.

2. Click Create Water Placer. This drops one placer actor into your level. You only ever need one per level, and the button disappears once it exists.

3. Set your sea level. Type the Z height where water should meet land, or click Suggest and let Geomancer analyse the terrain.

What Suggest actually does

It runs a flood from the map boundary to find edge-connected seabed, falling back to an elevation histogram on landlocked maps. It only applies a value when it is confident. If it is not, it leaves your number alone and says so — it will not guess quietly. Review the value it gives you before generating.

4. Click Generate All Water. Ocean, then lakes, then rivers — in that order, because each depends on the last. A progress dialog appears for anything slow, and you can cancel it; cancelling removes what was placed rather than leaving you half a world.

That is the whole basic loop. Everything below is refinement.

Reading the panel

The status line tells you where you are and what to do next. Grey means "next step", green means done, blue means partially placed, red means the last run failed with a reason.

The counts — 1 ocean · 12 lakes · 40 rivers — are swept from the level itself, not remembered, so they stay honest after a reload.

Toasts appear only when something needs your attention — a failure, a warning, a cancellation. Success is quiet by design.

The three sliders

ControlWhat it does
RiversFew, large ↔ many, small. Auto scales to your map; untick to set it yourself.
LakesLarger minimum area gives fewer, bigger lakes.
MeandersStraight ↔ winding. Cosmetic only — steep reaches stay straight regardless.

One thing worth knowing

Sliders save when you release the mouse. If you nudge one with the arrow keys, click something else before closing the panel — keyboard changes are not saved until another action triggers a save.

Advanced → Run stages individually

Four buttons for when you want control over each stage:

  • Spawn Ocean
  • Detect & Spawn Lakes — does both steps in one click.
  • Trace Rivers — draws river paths as debug lines and spawns nothing. Use it to preview before committing.
  • Spawn Rivers — creates the actual river bodies along those traced paths.

Note the asymmetry: lakes detect and spawn together, rivers are two separate steps. That is deliberate — river paths are worth previewing.

The two surfaces

The panel is not everything. The placer actor carries ten more actions that the panel does not show, including Analyse Terrain, per-type removal (Remove Ocean, Remove Lakes, Remove Rivers), debug overlays and analysis clearing.

To reach them: select the Water Placer in the World Outliner and use the standard Details panel.

The Geomancer panel is the streamlined path; the Outliner is the full one. If a tooltip mentions something you cannot find in the panel, that is where it lives.

Landscape carving

Carving cuts river beds into the terrain so water sits in a channel rather than on a plane. It is off by default and you do not need it — the tool places water correctly either way.

How it works: river carving uses Landscape Patch — a chain of localised circle patches along each river, written to a dedicated edit layer. It is not a full-landscape merge, which is what makes it safe to use on large terrain and easy to remove.

The large-landscape guard. Carving very large landscapes can exhaust video memory on cards below roughly 12 GB, and in the worst case hang the GPU. Geomancer detects that case, skips the carve, spawns the water anyway, and tells you. If you want to proceed regardless, tick Force Carve On Large Landscape — but save first, and know that it resets on reload so it can never quietly persist.

If you disable the Landscape Patch plugin

Carve data in a saved level is lost the next time that level is saved. This only matters if you have used carving.

When something goes wrong

Geomancer's warnings are written to tell you what to do. The common ones:

What it saysWhat to do
Could not confidently detect a sea level (no clear coastline)Your map may have no ocean, or no edge-connected seabed. Set Sea Level manually.
No Landscape actor found in the current levelAdd a landscape, or check you are in the right level.
Lake detection found no lakesLower Minimum Lake Area or Minimum Depth, and confirm Sea Level is right — depressions below sea level are ocean, not lakes.
Terrain Flow found no channel headsLower Min Catchment Area to seed more rivers, or check the terrain actually has relief above sea level for water to drain down.
Failed to spawn Water Body OceanThe Water plugin is not enabled. Edit → Plugins, search "Water".
Skipped landscape carving — this landscape exceeds the safe merge sizeWorking as intended. See Landscape carving.
Terrain analysis produced no usable coastlineCheck Sea Level actually intersects your terrain, and try a finer Ocean Analysis Cell Size.

Nothing happens when I click a button. Buttons grey out without a placer. Click Find to locate an existing one, or Create Water Placer.

If none of that helps, send a bug report — the template is short and it saves a round trip.

What it does not do

  • Generate terrain. It reads your landscape; it does not make one.
  • Run at runtime. Editor-only, deliberately.
  • Waterfalls. Not detected or authored.
  • Caves or overhangs. The analysis assumes one surface height per point.
  • Shore materials on rivers. Ocean and lakes only; rivers use the engine default.
